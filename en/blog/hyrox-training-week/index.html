How to train for HYROX: a real week, session by session

HYROX mixes 8 km of running with 8 strength stations. Running alone won't do it, and neither will lifting: you need both in the same week without one eating into the other. Here's how a real plan splits it.

Nov 2, 2026 · 4 min read

What race day looks like

The format never changes: run 1 km, do a station, eight times over. The stations, in order, are SkiErg (1,000 m), sled push (50 m), sled pull (50 m), burpee broad jumps (80 m), rowing (1,000 m), farmers carry (200 m), sandbag lunges (100 m) and wall balls (100 reps). Weights depend on your division.

So you need three things: an aerobic engine to string together 8 km while breaking rhythm every kilometer, strength to move the sled and hold your grip, and practicing the movements on tired legs.

The three pieces of a HYROX week

  • Strength with legs and pulling first. Squats, deadlifts and rows are what drive the sled, the lunges and your grip on the farmers carry.
  • Two runs with different goals: a tempo run to get used to running while uncomfortable, and a conversational long run to build your base.
  • Race movements at the end of strength days: sled, farmers carry, burpees or the rower, in short blocks and already tired, like on race day.

Why it's built this way

Three strength days, two running days. People coming from the gym usually have strength to spare and not much running, so running gets its own days instead of whatever is left over.

Legs and pulling outweigh pushing. In HYROX you push the sled with your legs and pull it with your back; the bench press barely shows up. So there's more squatting, deadlifting and rowing than pressing.

Race movements go last. Sled drags, med ball slams or burpees close the strength sessions in 30-40 second blocks. You practice them on tired legs, as in the race, without spoiling your technique on the main lifts.

The tempo run is split into blocks. Three 10-minute blocks in Zone 4 with 2 easy minutes between: sustained effort close to the race without running the whole thing.

The long run starts with an easy test. The first 30 conversational minutes measure distance and average heart rate. With that, the plan sets the next weeks' paces instead of guessing them.

Common HYROX prep mistakes

  • Training only the stations. They're the visible part, but a big share of race time is spent running.
  • Full simulations every week. They pile up fatigue and leave little room to build strength and base. Train the pieces separately.
  • Forgetting grip. The farmers carry and the sled punish it. Dumbbell carries and rows train it without extra sessions.
  • Arriving tired. The last weeks drop the volume and keep some intensity so you reach the start line fresh.
