Deload week: what it is, when to do it and what a real one looks like

Training less on purpose so you can perform better afterwards. It sounds odd, but the deload week is one of the most useful tools to keep making strength progress. Here's a real one.

Nov 2, 2026 · 3 min read

What a deload week is

It's a short period, usually a week, where you train less than usual to recover and come back performing better. It's not time off: you still train, just with less total load.

Recent work with strength coaches (Bell et al.) describes it as a planned reduction in training demand. It's most often done every few weeks, for a few days, mainly by cutting volume (fewer sets) and effort (more reps in reserve) rather than the weight on the bar.

Signs it's time

  • Sets that used to have reps to spare now take you to your limit.
  • Your weights stall or drop for several sessions in a row.
  • You sleep worse, struggle to get going or have nagging aches.
  • You've been adding load for many weeks without a break.

A real deload week

The week before, this powerlifter (woman, 27, training 4 days a week) completed every session, but at her limit: effort 9 out of 10 in every session. 2trAIn answered with this week:

What changes and what stays

The four days and the main lifts stay. Squat, bench, deadlift and overhead press are all still there. No need to drop them; what comes down is how hard they push you.

Effort drops, technique doesn't. Monday's cue is to leave 2–3 reps in reserve; Thursday's, clean technical sets. The deadlift goes to 4 × 3: few reps, done well.

It's a chance to sharpen up. Tuesday's bench has a pause on the chest. With less fatigue, it's a good moment to polish technique.

It comes with a reason. The plan doesn't cut the load because the calendar says so; it cuts it because the previous week was at her limit. If she'd had room to spare, the load would have kept going up.

How to do one yourself

  • Keep your main lifts and your training days.
  • Drop a set per exercise (for example, from 4 to 3).
  • Finish every set with 2–3 reps in reserve.
  • Then go back to your normal week, without trying to make up for anything.
